Features

What Nomoads does on your phone

Nomoads blocks ad and tracker domains at the DNS level across every app. Each feature below describes a specific capability, who it helps, and a concrete example.

System-wide ad blocking

Intercepts DNS requests from all apps via a local VPN and blocks known ad domains. Useful for mobile gamers and social media users who see ads inside apps, not just browsers—for example, blocking in-game ad network requests in a free puzzle app.

On-device privacy

DNS filtering and blocklist matching happen entirely on your phone—no proxy server receives your traffic. Privacy-conscious users can browse without a third party logging domain lookups; your DNS queries never leave the device for filtering.

Adjustable protection levels

Pick from Basic through Ultimate blocklist presets, or add custom blocklist URLs. Parents can switch to a stricter level for everyday filtering; casual users can stay on Basic for standard ad blocking.

Query stats and activity log

See how many DNS queries were blocked in the last 24 hours, a percent-blocked rate, an hourly chart, and the top blocked domains. Open the query log to search allowed and blocked lookups—counts stay on your phone and are not uploaded.

Advanced category blocklists

Turn on extra lists for fakes and scams, popup ads, piracy sites, gambling, social networks, and NSFW content. Each list can be refreshed independently so you can block phishing shops without also blocking social apps.

Per-app exclusions

Exclude specific apps from DNS filtering when a service breaks under blocking. Banking app users, for example, can exclude their bank's app so login flows are unaffected while ads stay blocked everywhere else.

Domain allowlist

Whitelist trusted domains that should never be blocked. News readers who support ad-funded sites can allowlist a publisher's domain so paywalled content loads while trackers on other sites stay blocked.

Low-overhead DNS engine

Uses a hash-based domain lookup engine designed for fast DNS resolution with minimal battery use. Daily phone users notice fewer ad loads without the slowdown that heavier content filters can cause.

FAQ

Nomoads FAQ

Answers to common questions about how Nomoads works, what it blocks, and how it handles your data.

What is Nomoads?

Nomoads is a mobile ad blocker for iOS and Android that filters ads and trackers at the DNS level across every app on your device.

Unlike browser-only extensions, Nomoads uses a local VPN to intercept DNS requests from all apps. When an app tries to reach a known ad or tracker domain, Nomoads blocks the lookup before the connection is made.

Does Nomoads send my browsing data to a server?

No. All DNS filtering and blocklist matching happen on your phone.

Nomoads uses a local VPN interface only to access DNS traffic on your device. Your queries are checked against on-device blocklists—they are not forwarded to Nomoads or any third-party server for filtering.

Why does Nomoads need VPN permission?

iOS and Android require VPN permission for any app that filters DNS traffic system-wide.

The VPN permission lets Nomoads inspect DNS requests from every app. Nomoads does not route your internet traffic through a remote VPN server—it only uses the local VPN channel to block ad domains on-device.

What types of content does Nomoads block?

Nomoads blocks ad networks, trackers, and malware domains listed in its blocklists.

The Basic plan includes a standard ad-blocking list. Premium adds protection levels plus advanced category blocklists you can toggle separately—fakes and scams, popup ads, piracy, gambling, social networks, and NSFW—and the ability to import custom blocklist URLs. You can also allowlist specific domains you want to keep accessible.

What does the Stats screen show?

Stats shows total DNS queries, how many were blocked, the percent blocked, a 24-hour activity chart, and the top blocked domains.

You can also open a query log to search allowed and blocked lookups. Counts and logs stay on your phone—they are not uploaded to Nomoads or a third-party analytics service.

What are advanced blocklists?

Advanced blocklists are optional category lists you can turn on or off, including fakes and scams, popup ads, anti-piracy, gambling, social networks, and NSFW content.

Each list shows how many domains it contains and can be refreshed on its own. Use them when you want stricter filtering than the Basic ad list without changing your overall protection level.

Can Nomoads block ads inside apps, not just browsers?

Yes. Because Nomoads filters DNS at the system level, it blocks ad domains in games, social apps, and any other app that loads ads over the network.

Browser extensions only work inside one browser. Nomoads intercepts DNS requests from all apps, so in-game ad networks and in-app banner trackers are blocked the same way as web ads.

What is the difference between Basic and Premium?

Basic is free and includes standard ad blocking. Premium ($2.99/month) adds four extra protection levels, custom blocklist URLs, advanced category blocklists, and per-app exclusions.

Basic covers everyday ad and tracker blocking for most users. Premium is for people who want stricter filtering (e.g., blocking gambling sites), custom lists, or fine-grained control over which apps are filtered.

Will Nomoads break my banking or work apps?

Most apps work normally. If an app has issues, you can exclude it from filtering in Premium settings.

Some apps with strict network checks may behave differently under DNS filtering. Nomoads lets Premium users exclude specific apps so those apps bypass the filter while protection stays active everywhere else.

Is Nomoads available on iPhone?

Yes. Nomoads is available on the App Store for iPhone and on Google Play for Android.

Download Nomoads from the App Store or Google Play. Both versions use on-device DNS filtering via Apple's Network Extension (iOS) or Android's VpnService.

How Nomoads handles your privacy

Nomoads processes DNS queries on your device instead of routing them through cloud servers. The app cannot see your full browsing history because it only inspects domain names at the DNS layer—and that inspection happens locally.

  • No cloud filtering servers Blocklist matching runs on your phone, not on Nomoads infrastructure
  • Stats stay on your phone Query counts and the activity log are stored on-device and are never uploaded
  • No third-party analytics on traffic Block counts are stored on-device only and are not tied to your identity
  • Public blocklist sources Filter lists follow open, community-maintained formats you can inspect
